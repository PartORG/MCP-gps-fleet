# Driver Behavior and Coaching

The fleet scores driving behaviour to reduce accidents, fuel consumption and
wear. Scores are used for coaching, not for punishment, unless a rule is broken
deliberately or repeatedly.

## What is measured

The driver score combines speeding (samples above 130 km/h, or above 80 km/h for
heavy trucks), harsh braking and acceleration, long idling and night driving.
Each trip is attributed to the driver who was assigned to that trip, which can
differ from the vehicle's regular driver for pool vehicles.

## Before talking to a driver

Make sure the data is real. A single speeding sample right next to a GPS jump is
usually a measurement error. Check whether the driver was actually driving that
trip, and whether there was an operational reason, such as an emergency
delivery requested by dispatch. Never confront a driver with a single sample.

## Coaching ladder

First event in 90 days: the coordinator mentions it in an informal conversation.
Second event: a documented coaching session that reviews the trips together with
the driver. Third event: an eco-driving or safety training course. Deliberate
violations, such as using a GPS jammer or speeding above 160 km/h, go directly
to the fleet manager.

## Idling

Drivers often idle to heat or cool the cab during breaks. Vehicles with a
parking heater should use it instead; it uses about a tenth of the fuel. Idling
while waiting at a customer's loading dock is not the driver's fault and should
be raised with the customer when it happens regularly.

## Driving hours

EU regulation 561/2006 applies to vehicles above 3.5 tonnes: a maximum of 9 hours
of driving per day (10 hours twice a week) and a 45-minute break after 4.5 hours
of driving. Trips that are much longer than 4.5 hours without a stop on a heavy
vehicle should be checked against the tachograph.
